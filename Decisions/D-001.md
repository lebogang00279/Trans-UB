# D-001 — Offline Payment and Physical Handover

## Decision

TRANS-UB will NOT process online payments. All payments and physical handovers will be arranged offline between the buyer and seller, outside the platform.

The system will support the ordering and transaction-recording process, but it will not handle, store, verify, or transfer money between users. The platform will record the order, its status, and the confirmation of handover, while the actual payment and exchange of goods or services will take place between the two students.

## Rationale

Integrating online payments would introduce significant technical complexity, security requirements, regulatory considerations, and potential liability that are beyond the scope of a semester project. The team also has limited time, resources, and access to external payment services.

Students already use existing methods such as cash and mobile money to conduct transactions. Keeping payment outside the platform therefore allows TRANS-UB to support the existing behaviour of student buyers and sellers rather than attempting to replace established payment methods.

Physical handover will also remain outside the platform because many products sold by students, such as textbooks, clothes, furniture, food, and accessories, may require the buyer and seller to arrange a suitable meeting point and, in some cases, allow the buyer to inspect the item before accepting it.

The platform can still provide accountability without handling money. By maintaining an order record and requiring the seller to mark an order as Delivered and the buyer to confirm it as Completed, TRANS-UB can establish whether a transaction was completed. Only completed transactions will be eligible for reviews, helping to maintain the credibility of the reputation system. This is consistent with the project's existing workflow, where a completed order is the basis for submitting a review.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Full online payment integration using services such as Stripe/PayPal | Rejected | Too complex for the semester, introduces additional security and regulatory requirements, and creates financial liability for the platform. |
| Mobile money integration | Rejected | Different mobile-money services may require different integrations, while payment-related fraud and transaction handling would increase system complexity. |
| Escrow service | Rejected | Would require the platform to hold and release users' money, creating additional financial, security, and legal responsibilities that are not feasible for the project. |
| No online payments | **Selected** | Keeps the system focused on its core purpose of connecting student buyers and sellers while supporting the existing way students conduct transactions. |

## Consequences

This decision introduces a dependency on the honesty and cooperation of both the buyer and seller for completing the transaction. Since TRANS-UB does not process payment, the system cannot independently confirm that money was exchanged.

To address this limitation, the system will implement a two-party confirmation process:

1. Buyer places an order.
2. Seller accepts the order and arranges the physical handover.
3. Seller marks the order as Delivered.
4. Buyer confirms that the item/service was received.
5. The order changes to Completed.
6. Only then can the buyer submit a review.

The system will maintain an order history and status record that can be used when handling reported disputes. The platform will also provide clear guidance that payment and physical handover occur directly between students and that TRANS-UB does not guarantee the safety of the transaction.
